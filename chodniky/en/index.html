Pavements and road surfaces

From the archival photograph to a repeatable material.

Start with readable visual evidence

First check whether the original resolves individual elements and their arrangement. If not, reject the crop as reconstruction input. Colour, dimensions, new cracks and hidden detail remain hypotheses. Older examples combined colour inference, rectification and synthesis; the later Kostelní trial used a saved, specific input crop.

Making a repeatable texture

Two closed periodic cuts find low-error paths through overlaps. Three-pixel local feathering joins the transitions. This does not mirror the pattern or blur the whole image. The resulting period is smaller and need not be square.

What was checked

All 27 textures have recorded visual checks of seams and corners. Every entry now shows a single 1 × 1 tile and its adjustable repeat at the same time, making seams and conspicuously recurring stones, stains or damage quick to spot. Repetition visibility is assessed separately. The checks do not establish physical scale, historical colour or compatibility between different variants.
